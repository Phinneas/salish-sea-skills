# Volunteer Onboarding

Treat onboarding as a risk-managed relationship, not an information dump. A volunteer should know what the role is, who is served, what good performance looks like, what they may not do, how to get help, and how the organization will protect their dignity and safety.

## Prerequisites

No connectors required — runs on Claude alone. You supply the mission, role definition, and policies described below; pasted notes or common document formats are fine.

## Inputs

Accept the mission, volunteer role, tasks and boundaries, schedule, audience served, location, supervisor, accessibility needs, safeguarding/safety policies, screening requirements, training materials, systems access, and first-shift date.

## Workflow

1. **Define the role.** Write a role card with purpose, outcomes, recurring tasks, time commitment, location, supervisor, prerequisites, prohibited activities, safety risks, accessibility notes, and a clear “not a substitute for staff” boundary.
2. **Intake for fit.** Collect contact, availability, skills, motivation, language/access needs, emergency contact where appropriate, and consent for communications. Ask only for information needed to place and support the person.
3. **Screen proportionately.** Apply role-based checks consistently. For roles involving children, vulnerable adults, finances, transport, or sensitive data, identify the organization’s applicable policy and jurisdiction-specific requirements for verification; do not invent a universal legal rule.
4. **Orient to mission and conduct.** Deliver a short live or recorded orientation covering mission, people served, dignity, inclusion, confidentiality, boundaries, conflicts, incident reporting, attendance, communication, and escalation. Require acknowledgment, not just a file download.
5. **Train to demonstration.** Teach the actual workflow, then ask the volunteer to demonstrate or shadow it. Record trainer, date, result, accommodations, and retraining trigger. Never grant system or client access before the readiness check.
6. **Place and schedule.** Match availability and skills to a named shift/assignment. Send a first-shift brief with arrival, dress/equipment, contact, parking/access, cancellation route, and what to bring.
7. **Support the first 30 days.** Schedule a first-shift check-in, week-two pulse, and day-30 review. Capture what worked, concerns, training gaps, recognition, and whether the role should be adjusted.
8. **Close the loop.** Maintain a lightweight status record: inquiry, intake, screening, orientation, trained, active, paused, exited. Remove access promptly on pause/exit and retain only necessary records.

## Output

Return a role card, intake fields, screening decision tree, orientation agenda, training checklist with demonstration, first-shift brief, 30-day follow-up plan, status tracker, and message templates. Mark each requirement `required`, `role-dependent`, or `optional`.

## Guardrails

Do not promise employment, benefits, confidentiality beyond policy, or safety outcomes the organization cannot provide. Do not collect sensitive data by default. Do not treat volunteers as employees or independent contractors for legal purposes; flag classification questions for qualified local advice. Safeguarding, background checks, and mandatory reporting vary by jurisdiction and role.
