# Fit Assessment: Cascade Watershed Alliance × Environmental Education Local Grants Program (EPA Region 10)

**Assessed:** 2026-08-28 · **Opportunity #:** Not stated in source (national program: EPA-EE) · **Deadline:** January 6, 2020 (illustrative — this dry run uses an archived cycle; see the NOFO decode's source note)

## Recommendation: Go with gaps
Cascade Watershed Alliance is eligible and has a genuinely strong
programmatic match — youth stream monitoring across three schools maps
directly onto both a required educational priority (Community Projects) and
a required environmental priority (clean and safe water). The real risk
isn't fit, it's execution capacity: the org has never managed a subaward
(a mandatory, points-bearing mechanism in this NOFO), its cost-share funds
cover the match with zero margin at the top of the award range, and none of
the required planning artifacts (logic model, timeline, partnership
letters) exist yet. All three are buildable before submission if there's
enough runway — none are structural disqualifiers.

## 2. Mission/program fit
Strong, direct match. The org's existing youth water-quality monitoring
program *is* the environmental-education activity this NOFO funds, not a
program that would need reshaping to fit — it already addresses "clean and
safe water" (environmental priority) through hands-on monitoring, and
"Community Projects" (educational priority) through the school
partnerships. This is the strongest part of the org's case.

**Weighted read:** The org is strong on the highest-weighted content itself
(the 20-point educational/environmental "What" and "How" sub-criteria) but
weak specifically on the Subaward Selection & Management sub-criterion (3
pts) and the related Project Evaluation subaward-monitoring sub-criterion (3
pts) — a combined 6 points that also happens to sit on top of a mandatory
threshold requirement. That combination makes the subaward gap the single
highest-priority item to close, ahead of the lower-stakes missing planning
documents.

## 5. Gap list (ranked by criterion weight and threshold risk)
1. **No subaward plan** — needed for the mandatory 25%-subaward threshold
   requirement plus ~6 review points (How: Subaward Management, 3 pts;
   Project Evaluation: Subaward monitoring, 3 pts). Identify 2-3 candidate
   sub-recipients now (e.g., a small stewardship micro-grant routed through
   the partner land trust, a school environmental-club fund) and draft the
   management/oversight plan — this is the single highest-priority gap.
2. **No signed Partnership Letters** — worth 5 points. Convert the informal
   school-district and land-trust relationships into actual letters of
   commitment, or explicitly build the case for executing without formal
   partners if letters can't be secured in time.
3. **No Logic Model or Timeline** — worth 10 points combined. Not a
   structural gap — buildable from the existing program design in the prep
   window, just needs to actually get drafted.
4. **Thin diversity/underserved-community evidence** — worth 5 points. Pull
   real demographic data on the 3 partner schools; if they don't already
   serve an underserved population, consider whether outreach can
   credibly expand to one before claiming this criterion.
5. **Cost-share margin is zero at the award ceiling** — not a scored
   criterion directly, but a real execution risk. Target the $50,000-65,000
   end of the award range rather than the $100,000 ceiling, where the
   $25,000 match comfortably clears instead of exactly meeting it.
6. **No formal outcomes-tracking plan** — worth 6 points combined
   (educational + environmental tracking). State a concrete measurement
   plan rather than relying on informal program monitoring.

## Bottom line
Apply — but at the lower-middle of the award range ($50,000-65,000, not the
$100,000 ceiling), and only if there's enough runway before the deadline to
actually build the subaward plan, partnership letters, logic model, and
timeline. The programmatic fit is genuinely strong; the gaps are almost all
in application-prep artifacts and one real administrative capacity question
(subawards), not in whether this org should be doing this work. If the
subaward plan can't be credibly built before the deadline, that alone is
reason enough to skip this cycle and target the next one with more lead
time.
